# Presentation and guideline: the Energy Cyber Society in ten minutes

For presenting to someone who has never seen it. Spoken script with timings; the bracketed
lines are what to click. Total about nine and a half minutes at a calm pace. Open the demo
before you start and keep the Gazette in a second tab.

## 0:00 – 0:45 · What it is

"This is a small town. Fourteen residents live in it, and every one of them is an AI agent
with one job: reading the day's cybersecurity news about the energy sector, worldwide, United
States first. They read only from a fixed list of credible sources, they argue with each other,
and they reduce what they read to a few decision-ready lines. You can watch them, click any of
them to ask a question, and check every claim they make against its source. The point is not
more data. The point is less, with the reasoning shown."

[Let the town run for a few seconds; point at two residents talking; point at the transcript.]

## 0:45 – 1:45 · The residents and why there are fourteen

"Each building is a job. The Reporter writes what happened. The Analyst asks who and how, and
whether an attack touched the physical control systems, which is the single most important
question in this sector. The Media Critic asks what each outlet's bias is. The Wide-Angle reads
everything that is not about energy and asks what could spill over. The Contrarian asks who
was *not* affected. The Verifier audits everything after the others. The Librarian keeps the map
of where to look. Then the second row: Counsel reads the law in the original, the Archivist
keeps the case history, the Ombudsman audits the residents themselves, the Operator says what
a recommendation means on a night shift, the Economist counts only numbers with a source, the
Geopolitical Analyst maps conflicts onto the grid by layer, and the Technologist follows the AI
models and their energy use. Their names are invented; click a building and it says so."

[Click a building. Show "About the name".]

## 1:45 – 3:15 · The final output

"If you only have one minute, this is the tab." [Click *Final output: Executive brief*.]
"Ten threats for an energy executive, ranked. Each line says why it matters at board level,
your exposure, the action, how confident we are, and the source, as a link. The brief is
unsigned, on purpose: the residents assemble the picture; a person evaluates it and signs.
Notice the line 'not on the list': this week no US energy operator was confirmed breached, and
the system says so rather than inventing a threat to fill the space."

[Click one source link; it opens the outlet. Come back.]

"The Summary is the same week in bullets, with the sample size: how many feeds, how many
stories, how many survived. Small by design."

## 3:15 – 4:30 · How a claim is checked

[Click *Briefing board*, then one item, to open the full analysis.]
"Every story that matters becomes a record: what happened, the energy subsector, IT versus OT,
actor, CVE, impact, and two separate grades: how important, and how much to trust. They are
different columns on purpose. This item is critical and poorly confirmed; another is fully
confirmed and minor. Scroll: the Verifier's verdict, the cross-check that set the outlet's
text next to the primary document, the pulled excerpts, the breach anatomy with the layers to
add, and every flag raised about it."

## 4:30 – 5:30 · Cyber Command and the rules

[Click *Cyber Command*.]
"Any time a resident writes a sentence without a source, rests an item on one outlet,
contradicts a primary document, or says something that is not in the fetched bundle, it is
reported here the moment it happens, and never deleted. You can paste any claim and verify it
against everything the system read. If it matches nothing, it says NOT IN THE BUNDLE."
[Click *Rules*.] "Thirty-two rules the residents work under. Rule 25: never invent. Rule 9:
'I cannot verify this' is a required answer."

## 5:30 – 6:30 · The world and the facilities

[Click the newsstand to open the Gazette in the other tab.]
"The same week as a newspaper: lead story, columns, the ten threats, a world desk by continent
that says where the feeds were silent. Written by AI residents from the allowlist; the names
are fictional." [Back to the town; click the Substation.] "Twelve facilities on the map, one
per subsector: what runs there in IT, OT and the embedded layer, where it breaks, how a breach
actually unfolds, this week's live cases, and historical cases, labelled as background until
verified."

## 6:30 – 7:30 · Sources, languages, history

[Click *Where to look*, then *Sources & references*.]
"Thirty-eight feeds in nine languages, each with a bias note, and the places a person must
read by hand: journals, paywalled standards, members-only sharing bodies. Every URL the
system read or cited is listed and searchable." [Click *History*.] "Every run is archived in
full and can be reopened as its own edition. Nothing is overwritten."

## 7:30 – 8:30 · Money, energy, and what needs a person

[Click *Cost & energy*, change the model, type an energy assumption.]
"What a run costs by stage and by resident, by model, with your own questions logged. Energy
per token is not published by the provider, so the tab only shows what *your* assumption
implies, labelled as yours." [Click *Bank*.] "Spend run by run, projected month, and a budget
with a warning." [Click *Human touch*.] "Twelve things that still need a person: deciding what
matters, reading the law, attributing responsibly, signing the brief, owning mistakes."

## 8:30 – 9:30 · Honesty about limits, and the ask

[Click *Limitations*, then *Notifications*.]
"It is a snapshot, not live. It cannot see what nobody reported. Its historical timeline is
unverified until the Archivist checks it. The pipeline has been designed to run twice a day
unattended, and the honest status is that the first unattended run has not happened yet.
The bell shows what you missed while you were away. The review file in the repository lists
every known flaw with its fix."

"The ask: a public repository so the page is live, an API key so the residents run on their
own, and one person willing to read the sources behind ten lines and sign."

## Guideline for presenters

- Never say "the agents found". Say "the residents report, from these sources", and click one.
- If asked "is this true?", open the full analysis and read the trust line and the verdict.
- If asked about a country or sector with nothing shown, open the world desk or the sample
  line and read the sentence that says the feeds were silent.
- If asked who the people are, say they are invented, and show the building note.
- If asked what it costs, open the Bank; if asked about energy, say "not published" first.
- Do not promise the schedule until an archived run with model "scheduled" exists.
